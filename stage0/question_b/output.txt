FINDINGS:
1. Higher optimisation levels gave big speedups, mostly from -O0 to -O2. -O3 added nothing, and at -O2/-O3 the loop was essentially optimised away, so those numbers are below the timer’s resolution.
2. Recursion was always slower than the loop because of function-call overhead, until -O2 partly converted it into a loop.
3. Windows was about 2.8× faster for the loop but slower for recursion, so performance depends on the architecture, calling convention, and compiler, not just CPU speed.
